    Method & limits

    SAGA audits infrastructure, not people. It produces a list of places a city should inspect, never a ticket.

    Dashcam frameBDD100K, 1280×720, day & night, clear to snow.
    Semantic segmentationSegFormer-B2, Cityscapes-pretrained, fine-tuned on 6,500 BDD images.
    Ground-contact auditFor each object: what surface is directly beneath it and beside it?
    Priority mapFindings are geolocated with the clip's GPS and ranked for repair.

    Report from a phone

    Residents open the SAGA mobile app, take a photo, and get the audit back in plain text first (so it works with VoiceOver), then the overlay, then "Did I get this right?"

    • 👍 on a finding: confirmed (a true positive). 👎: rejected (a false alarm). 👎 on "nothing found": missed (a barrier the model did not see).
    • These answers become field labels. They measure real-world precision per flag and pick the photos most worth annotating for the next training run.
    • Location comes from the phone's GPS, or a cross street the resident types.
    • Privacy: people and the plate band of every vehicle are blurred before the photo is saved; no account, name or phone number is stored.

    Audit rules

    • Sidewalk obstruction a car, truck, bus, motorcycle or bicycle whose contact strip (6 px below its lowest edge) is ≥ 60% sidewalk and which has ≥ 40% sidewalk beside its lowest rows.
    • Pedestrian in roadway a person whose contact strip is ≥ 60% road and who has road beside their feet.
    • No visible sidewalk ≥ 5% building, ≥ 10% road and ≤ 0.5% sidewalk in the frame, with the curb zones visible (≤ 25% parked vehicles at the left/right edge) and no highway barriers (≤ 3% wall + fence). Without the last two checks, 183 of 1,000 test frames fired, mostly NYC streets where parked cars hide the sidewalk.
    • Only nearby objects count (minimum height ≈ 70 px at full resolution), and objects cut off by the frame edge are skipped. Both rules were added after visual review showed perspective errors on distant cars.
    • Priority score: 40 for no visible sidewalk, 25 per obstruction (max 50), 15 per pedestrian in roadway (max 30), +15 when pedestrians share a street with no visible sidewalk. This score is a heuristic for ranking, not a validated measure.

    Known limits

    • Rules are checked against labels, not reality. Audit agreement measures how well the model reproduces the audit that the human masks would give. It does not prove each flag is a real ADA violation.
    • No crosswalk or curb-ramp class. People crossing at a crosswalk also count as "in roadway"; curb ramps need a different dataset (Project Sidewalk + Mapillary).
    • One frame is not a street. "No visible sidewalk" can mean hidden by parked cars. Aggregating many drives per block would fix this.
    • Privacy. BDD images show faces and plates. A public deployment must blur both before display.
    • Licence. BDD100K permits educational, research and non-profit use.
